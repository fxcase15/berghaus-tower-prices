How this was built

Sources. Areas are NEN 2580 usable floor areas (GBO) from NEN2580 type B meting Bergaus A6. Sale prices and dates come from Kadaster koopsominformatie for postcode 1062 LE (49 transfers, Jul 2025 – Aug 2026). The 80 units match the 80 apartment rights (272–351) on floors 5–12 in Concept splitsingstekening A6.

Duplexes. W3/W3A and W4 span two floors. Their area is the sum of both levels, for example W3 = 34.15 m² entrance level + 68.07 m² upper level. For the model they count as the floor in between (e.g. 7.5).

Model. An ordinary least-squares fit of € per m² on apartment type, floor and months since July 2025, computed live from the 49 sales. "Vs. model" is the sale price relative to that fit.

Façade length. This is the exterior wall length of each unit in the simplified 3D model, used as a stand-in for glass. It is approximate (about ±1 m), and the rounded glass corners are drawn as square.

Rental block in 3D. House numbers, types and GBO areas of the 205 rental homes come from the same NEN 2580 plans (floors 1–4 ring, floors 5–12 second tower). Eight tower units whose labels could not be read are filled in from the repeating floor pattern and marked as inferred. The ring is drawn schematically: each home sits on the correct side of the courtyard in plan order, with width proportional to its area. The rental tower layout follows the plans; its position next to our tower is approximate.

Rentals. The other Berghaus blocks are rental housing (288 homes in total, mostly mid-rent), so there are no Kadaster sale prices for them. The rental table lists only asking rents that are publicly visible on Funda or Huurmatcher, as last advertised. Floor is shown where the listing states it. Unit 551 appears twice because it was let in 2025 and re-advertised in 2026. The gross yield is the average rent × 12 divided by the average sale price per m² of the A6 tower; it ignores service costs, vacancy and the different mix of unit types.

Timing. Dates are notarial transfer dates. For new-build units the price was usually fixed earlier at contract signing, so the date effect understates real market movement.
